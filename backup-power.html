Questions

Battery backup FAQ

Does a home battery backup system need a permit?

Yes. Connecting batteries or a transfer panel to your home's wiring needs an electrical permit and inspection, and a battery system installed at your home can also need a City building permit. We apply for the electrical permit, book the inspection, and tell you at the site visit whether a building permit is needed.

Can I buy the EcoFlow equipment myself?

Yes. You can buy the equipment and we install it, or we can supply it on larger jobs. Tell us the model before you buy. We check that it is approved for use in Canada and suits your home. Equipment we supply is covered by our 2-year warranty and the manufacturer's warranty also applies. Equipment you buy yourself is covered by the manufacturer's warranty only; our warranty covers our workmanship. Full terms and exclusions are written on your quote.

What will a battery backup system power, and for how long?

That depends on the equipment you choose and what you connect to it, so we don't promise numbers before we've seen your home. At the site visit we run a load calculation, agree which circuits go on backup, and tell you what to expect.

What does a smart home panel or transfer switch do?

It moves the circuits you choose over to the battery during a power outage (automatically or with a manual switch, depending on the equipment), and stops the battery from feeding power back onto the utility lines. It is wired into your panel, so it is installed to code under permit.

Will I need a service upgrade to add battery backup?

Not necessarily. The load calculation tells us what your service can carry, and load management can sometimes help a battery system fit without a larger service. See load management.
