Capacity comes first

The most expensive mistake is installing chargers one owner at a time until the main service runs out. The second most expensive is upgrading the service when the building didn't need it.

We calculate the building's demand load to the Canadian Electrical Code, look at actual utility demand data, and show the board exactly how many stalls it can support today and what it would take to get further.

Load calculation
Spare capacity in kW, from your panel schedules and utility demand history.
EV energy management (EVEMS)
Lets several chargers share one circuit by managing when and how fast each one charges.
EV-ready stalls
A junction box or receptacle at each stall, so owners can add their own charger later.
Service upgrade, if needed
Sized from the load calculation and permitted. Larger services are designed with a professional engineer and coordinated with the utility. Our crew has hands-on experience installing services up to 1600 A.

Calgary: waiting for next intake

ChargeYYC (City of Calgary)

The pilot paid 50% of EV-ready infrastructure costs (up to $6,000 per stall, $90,000 maximum) and 50% of Level 2 chargers (up to $10,000), to a combined maximum of $100,000 per property. The last intake closed on November 18, 2025, and the City says it expects to take more applications in future.

  • The City's program page says the EV Charging Road Map must be completed by a Professional Engineer licensed in Alberta. We work with engineers and supply pricing and installation.
  • A building that already knows its capacity and stall plan can apply quickly when it reopens.

Common questions

Does our building need a new electrical service first?

Often not. A load calculation shows how much spare capacity the building has. Energy management lets several chargers share that capacity, which can delay or shrink a service upgrade.

Can one owner install a charger in their own stall?

In most condos the parkade electrical is common property, so the board has to approve it first. A building-wide plan usually costs less per stall than approving one-off installs.

Who pays for the electricity a charger uses?

That's a board decision. Networked chargers can track each user's energy so it can be billed back; simpler setups use a flat fee. We explain the options and their costs so the board can choose.

Do you write the ChargeYYC road map?

The City's ChargeYYC page says the road map must be completed by a Professional Engineer licensed in Alberta, so we work alongside an engineer and handle pricing and installation. The program is closed for now; we'll confirm the rules when it reopens.
